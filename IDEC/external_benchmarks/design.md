## Why the clean MNIST baselines differ

The reference reproduction uses the authors' released autoencoder and the original concatenation of 60,000 training and 10,000 test images. It scored 88.10% by Hungarian matching on those 70,000 images at seed 0 after 20,000 updates. The background comparison's 73.23% and the feature comparison's 73.63% are separate five-seed pixel-accuracy measurements on unseen test digits after fitting on 12,000 original training images. The different procedures also change the initialization, training budget and label mapping; their gap cannot be attributed to one factor.

For a direct full-data check, use the same 70,000 clean images, original order, transductive Hungarian scoring, 20,000 clustering updates and seed 0. Cross two starting recipes with two reconstruction targets. From the authors' pixel checkpoint, hold the initial weights and K-means centers fixed and vary only the fine-tuning target. From scratch, pretrain for 100 epochs on either pixels or selected features and retain that target during fine-tuning. The latter pair tests the full two-stage proposal. To fit the selector, permute all images with seed 126 before splitting into independent halves, then apply the learned basis in the original order. No class labels enter selection or optimization. The two contrasts answer different questions. The full-data check is single-seed and transductive, separate from the external held-out benchmark.
