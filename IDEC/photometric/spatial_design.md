# Extending the augmentation comparison

The initial four-arm design isolated grayscale brightness/contrast jitter and a fixed Gaussian pixel-loss prior. This extension was specified during that run, before inspecting its performance estimates or fitting spatial arms. It adds two standalone whole-image transformations. Geometric marks keep their assigned shape and position relative to the handwriting during training.

- **Translation.** Sample independent horizontal and vertical integer offsets uniformly from the legal offsets within three pixels of zero. Legal offsets preserve all nonzero input pixels within the image. Translating a marked image moves handwriting and mark together.
- **Rotation.** Sample uniformly from legal angles in {-12, -8, -4, 0, 4, 8, 12} degrees about the image center. Use bilinear sampling on a padded canvas and reject candidates with transformed intensity exceeding one millionth of full scale outside the central 28 by 28 crop. Always include the exact identity. Record eligibility, since marks near the border can restrict the available transformations.

For each transformation, train it alone and with the original Gaussian on marked training digits. Also train it alone on clean digits. These six arms share the existing clean and marked baselines and the Gaussian-only arm. No jitter is applied in the spatial arms. Use the same five seeds, split, detached marks, budget, ordinary unaugmented IDEC fine-tuning, training-only digit map, final shape probe and held-out tests from the whole-image jitter protocol. Computing the reconstruction target from the transformed view tests ordinary augmentation during pretraining.

For method choice compare all standalone candidates using the prespecified movement flip rate, shape flips, marked and clean digit accuracy, linear shape readout, and loss of accuracy when the whole test digit moves. Check whether a method's digit-accuracy gain also occurs when both training and testing use clean digits. Treat contradictory or seed-sensitive outcomes explicitly. The clean controls and within-image interventions assess two possible effects and do not decompose accuracy into causal mediation percentages.
