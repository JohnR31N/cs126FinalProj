# Adaptive Gaussian reconstruction on all 70,000 clean MNIST digits

Compare the final adaptive circular Gaussian with aligned saliency to ordinary IDEC on the transductive sample used by the original MNIST reproduction. Both students start from the same random initialization at seed 0 and learn their own pretraining weights. Concatenate the original training and test arrays in that order, scale pixel values by 255/50, pretrain for 100 epochs, fit ten K-means centers with 20 restarts, and take 20,000 ordinary IDEC updates. The weighted student uses an ordinary unsupervised autoencoder and K-means teacher trained on these same images at seed 126 to compute its saliency. The fixed Gaussian width is seven pixels and every image has its own intensity-based center. No synthetic backgrounds enter this comparison.

Score both final cluster assignments on all 70,000 fitting images with Hungarian ACC and normalized mutual information. Save the assignments and labels for CPU rescoring. This single-seed comparison matches sample, optimization budget and starting conditions between the two students. The historical 88.10% reproduction starts directly from the authors' already pretrained autoencoder and is reported as a separate reference. A difference between the two fresh students is conditional on this seed. Generalization to unseen digits is evaluated in the five-seed held-out study.
