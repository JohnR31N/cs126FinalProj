# Does keeping reconstruction during clustering increase the cost of irrelevant backgrounds?

This follow-up was specified before running the DEC comparisons. Earlier IDEC, clean-target, translation and Gaussian-pretraining results on this split are already known. Our primary question concerns the *difference in accuracy losses* after adding geometric backgrounds to DEC and IDEC. The proposed modification weights reconstruction by a fixed center prior, since the handwriting in this MNIST task is usually centered.

Use the previously fixed 12,000 training and 10,000 held-out images, five seeds, two detached symbols, and the original 96 grayscale templates. In each domain, train and test on its matching images. Compare clean with the first marked view of the same digits. A marked training digit keeps its assigned symbols. Two independent held-out redraws at the same sites change only symbol attributes. Reproduce the earlier generator and check all of its metadata against the archived evaluation file. Background attributes are sampled independently of digit labels. Legal placement sites depend on the handwriting.

For each seed and domain, fork DEC and IDEC from an identical autoencoder pretrained with uniform reconstruction and identical K-means centers. Use the same Adam settings, batches, target refreshes and 5,600 updates. DEC fine-tuning uses only the clustering KL term. Keep its positive multiplier at 0.1 in both branches to isolate the addition of pixel MSE. DEC's standard autoencoder initialization has already seen a reconstruction loss. Record accuracy at that shared initialization. Also include a separately labelled KL-only control starting from a random encoder and K-means centers, which skips all reconstruction and all pretraining updates. It measures a different training recipe.

The proposed Gaussian IDEC uses the same full marked targets and images as ordinary IDEC. Weight each squared pixel residual by a fixed mean-one Gaussian centered at (13.5, 13.5), with sigma 7 pixels. Test weighting in both stages as the primary modification, in pretraining only, and in fine-tuning only. Gaussian fine-tuning alone shares the ordinary initialization. Gaussian pretraining alone and weighting in both stages share the Gaussian initialization. Each method also gets a clean training and test control. Both Gaussian parameters were fixed in the earlier study. Calculate the mean Gaussian weight over digit ink and over added symbol pixels as a check on this positional assumption. Training has access only to each input image and its pixel coordinates.

For each model fit its cluster-to-digit mapping with training assignments and labels only after fitting. Freeze it across the clean, marked, and redrawn test views. The primary outcome is the paired loss in percentage points, clean-trained/clean-tested ACC minus marked-trained/marked-tested ACC. Within each seed compute the IDEC loss minus the DEC loss. A positive difference means IDEC deteriorated more. Also compute the ordinary IDEC loss minus the Gaussian loss, the Gaussian gain separately on marked and clean digits, and the effect of adding symbols only at test time to each frozen model. Report all five differences, their mean, sample SD, direction counts, and occupied clusters. Redraw flips measure a decision's sensitivity to symbol attributes. For the IDEC variants also measure the decoder response to the changed pixels using the archived projection readout. These measures describe behavior or information used for reconstruction; they cannot establish that the encoder has forgotten the symbols.

The DEC comparison isolates the fine-tuning objective conditional on a shared initialization in each domain. If DEC also declines, a loss from adding backgrounds is possible even during KL-only fine-tuning. An inconsistent or reversed interaction limits any claim that keeping reconstruction in clustering makes IDEC more vulnerable. The previously measured clean-target oracle remains a separate diagnostic of what the reconstruction target rewards.
